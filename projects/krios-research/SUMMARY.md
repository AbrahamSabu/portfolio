## The market in five bullets

1. **AI voice roleplay is already a commodity.** More than twenty named vendors compete in it, and G2 created an "AI Sales Roleplay" category in May 2026 whose top three products are Second Nature, Allego and Mindtickle. Two of those three are enablement platforms that bolted roleplay on. Gong launched Gong Enable on 25 February 2026. Salesforce ships Agentforce Sales Coach inside Sales Cloud. [Detail](./FULL-REPORT.md#23-where-the-category-is-heading)

2. **The money in in-person selling is going to the recording camp, not the roleplay camp.** Rilla and Siro raised USD 118.5m between them in 2025. Hyperbound and Second Nature raised USD 37m. Sacra reports Rilla at roughly USD 70m ARR in April 2026 with an average contract around USD 20,000 a year. No AI roleplay pure-play is close. [Detail](./FULL-REPORT.md#22-funding-scale-and-consolidation)

3. **The three-camp framing needs one correction.** Siro runs a dedicated door-to-door landing page, and Rilla now sells a roleplay module at rilla.com/roleplay. The claim that the field coaching camp leaves the doorstep untouched is falsifiable in one search. The narrower claim survives: neither is built around the doorstep, priced for a twenty-rep Irish crew, or localised for Ireland. [Detail](./FULL-REPORT.md#42-camp-two-field-and-in-person-coaching)

4. **The real competitor is nothing at all.** SPOTIO's State of Field Sales 2026 (6 March 2026, n=452) found 33% of field sales teams use zero AI tools, 41% report annual rep turnover of 50% or higher, and 40% take three months or more to ramp a new rep. That is the problem and the market in three numbers. [Detail](./FULL-REPORT.md#24-the-door-to-door-and-field-sales-industry)

5. **Ireland is a beachhead, not a market.** There is no published count of Irish D2D reps. Bottom-up from named agencies (Uniquely states 250-plus field sales professionals across UK and Ireland combined) the plausible Irish population is roughly 1,500 to 4,000 people, which is an inference and not a source. At realistic pricing that caps Irish ARR in the low hundreds of thousands for years. The tailwinds are real (34,088 grant-aided solar installs in 2025, 49,074 energy switches in December 2025 alone, 500,000-plus premises passed under the National Broadband Plan) but they do not change the arithmetic. [Detail](./FULL-REPORT.md#3-ireland)

## The three biggest threats

**1. Rilla and Siro have already claimed the doorstep in marketing, and one of them now sells practice.** Rilla has a roleplay product page live, an estimated USD 70m ARR, 2,000-plus accounts and Google Ventures behind a USD 68.5m Series B. Siro raised USD 50m in May 2025 and its press release names door-to-door roofers, solar installers and home security reps as its market. Both sit inside the field coaching budget Kríos wants a share of, both talk to the same manager, and neither needs to be excellent at rehearsal to make Kríos a second invoice that is hard to justify. The honest defence is not that they cannot do practice. It is that their practice is an add-on to a recording product priced for established American home services contractors, with no doorstep-specific design, no Irish voices and no Irish compliance story. That defence has maybe 24 months in it.

**2. The canvassing platform your buyer already pays for could absorb this as a USD 19 add-on.** SalesRabbit publishes Team at USD 59 per user per month, Pro at USD 49 annually, and add-ons like DataGrid AI and Weather at USD 19 per user per month. It already has a Learn training module and an Enterprise-tier Amplify Coaching feature. Knockbase is publishing blog posts anticipating real-time voice AI coaching for reps. None of them has shipped roleplay yet, and none of them has to be good at it. They only have to be adequate and already on the invoice. This is the largest displacement risk in the report and the reason to treat these companies as potential channel partners rather than rivals.

**3. Voice roleplay is becoming a feature, and Kríos has no structural moat yet.** Persona prompting, voice quality, scoring and manager dashboards are all replicable within a quarter by anyone with capital. ElevenLabs sells agent minutes at USD 0.08 and the models are getting cheaper. Gong, Salesforce, Mindtickle and Allego are all shipping roleplay inside larger products. In 24 months, "does it do AI roleplay" will be a checkbox, not a purchase decision. Everything durable about Kríos is currently potential rather than actual: the Irish and UK localisation is a temporary moat, and the genuinely defensible asset, a proprietary dataset of Irish and UK doorstep conversations, does not exist yet.

## The three biggest opportunities

**1. Nobody is answering the door-to-door query, and there is a brand new G2 category sitting half empty.** Every ranking roundup for AI sales roleplay covers inside-sales tools, because until now there was no door-to-door option to list. That is a finite outreach task with high leverage: the roundups feed the LLM answers. Meanwhile G2 introduced the AI Sales Roleplay category in May 2026, and new categories start thin, so a vendor that claims a profile and gathers genuine reviews this year can rank in a way that becomes impossible later. The existing SEO plan already names claiming G2 as the top action. This research says do it this month and name the category.

**2. Kríos is priced against the wrong camp.** Field sales buyers reportedly pay Rilla around USD 4,000 to 5,000 per seat per year. Inside sales buyers pay Kendo USD 660 per seat per year. Kríos removed a EUR 20 per seat price that was almost certainly below variable cost: at fifteen minutes of practice a day, a single rep burns roughly USD 24 a month in voice alone at the USD 0.08 per minute rate ElevenLabs publishes for agent minutes, before any LLM cost. The buyer for this product already pays field-camp prices for a coaching tool. There is far more room than EUR 20 suggests, and a published ladder around EUR 45 to EUR 75 per seat with explicit pooled minute allowances would beat both the old price and the current "book a demo" page.

**3. Irish regulation is a product feature, not a tax.** Ireland gives a 30-day cooling-off period on unsolicited doorstep sales, against 14 days online. The CRU requires an energy rep to show a photo ID card and hand over the doorstep checklist before pitching. Charities Institute Ireland publishes a face-to-face code of practice. Practice sessions involve no customer, so unlike Rilla and Siro, Kríos generates no customer personal data at all, which is a first-order objection in an Irish GDPR conversation. And the EU AI Act high-risk obligations for worker performance monitoring were deferred from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, giving Kríos sixteen months to be the vendor with an answer in writing while US competitors are not thinking about it. None of this is currently on the site.

## The one thing that would most change the outcome

**Get real Irish and UK doorstep conversations into the product, and build the objection taxonomy from them.**

Everything else in this report is a two-year advantage at best. Voice quality gets copied. Locale hints get copied. The doorstep framing gets copied the moment it is shown to work, and Siro has already copied the words. What cannot be copied is a corpus of actual conversations from actual Irish estates, linked to actual outcomes, that makes the Kríos prospect personas behave the way a real householder in Mallow or Tallaght behaves, and that lets a manager see which objection is costing their specific market money this month.

That is precisely what makes Rilla worth a reported USD 757m post-money: not the software, the dataset of in-person sales conversations. Kríos already has an analyse-call path in the backend and a privacy posture strong enough to ask for the data honestly. In 24 months, a Kríos that has that corpus is a category owner in these islands. A Kríos that does not is a nicely built roleplay app competing on price with a feature that lives inside somebody else's platform.
